# CPE 424: Computer Graphics & Visualization
## Topic: Lighting and the Phong Reflection Model

In 3D computer graphics, illumination models simulate how light interacts with virtual surface geometry to produce realistic visual shading.

The Phong Illumination Model calculates reflected light intensity I at a surface point as the sum of three distinct components:
I = I_ambient + I_diffuse + I_specular

1. Ambient Component (I_ambient):
   Simulates background indirect scattering of light in the environment:
   I_ambient = k_a * I_a (where k_a is ambient reflection coefficient).

2. Diffuse Component (I_diffuse):
   Follows Lambert's Cosine Law for rough, matte surfaces reflecting light equally in all directions:
   I_diffuse = k_d * I_l * max(0, N . L)
   where N is surface normal vector and L is unit vector toward the light source.

3. Specular Component (I_specular):
   Models shiny highlights reflecting in the direction of the reflection vector R:
   I_specular = k_s * I_l * max(0, R . V)^alpha
   where V is viewpoint vector, R is reflection vector 2(N . L)N - L, and alpha is surface shininess exponent. High alpha produces tight, mirror-like highlights.
