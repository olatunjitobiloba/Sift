# EIE 425: Applied Electromagnetic Field Theory
## Topic: Maxwell's Equations and Plane Wave Propagation

Maxwell's equations unify electric and magnetic phenomena in differential form:

1. Gauss's Law for Electrostatics:
   del . D = rho_v  (Electric displacement field divergence equals volume free charge density)
2. Gauss's Law for Magnetism:
   del . B = 0  (No isolated magnetic monopoles exist; magnetic flux lines are continuous closed loops)
3. Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction:
   del x E = - partial(B) / partial(t)  (Time-varying magnetic field induces a circulating electric field)
4. Ampere-Maxwell Circuital Law:
   del x H = J + partial(D) / partial(t)  (Conduction current J and displacement current partial(D)/partial(t) generate magnetic field)

Electromagnetic Wave Equation:
Taking the curl of Faraday's law in a lossless, source-free dielectric medium yields the vector wave equation:
del^2 E - mu * epsilon * partial^2(E) / partial(t^2) = 0
where wave propagation velocity is c = 1 / sqrt(mu_0 * epsilon_0) in free space.
